# VisionBridge — first-50 accounts: qualification framework

_Working doc. Agent research merges into §4 below._

## 1. The buy-now qualifier (all five must be true)

A big organization "would be interested" — that is not a customer. A customer in the next 90 days
satisfies **all five**:

1. **Budget line already exists.** They already pay for security (guards, screening, cameras,
   command center). New money is not required — the purchase displaces or extends an existing line.
2. **A burn, or a visible near-miss.** Someone got onto a roof, over a fence, through a gate, or the
   venue has a documented incident/threat history. Fear with a date on it releases budget faster than
   any ROI model.
3. **Multi-site leverage.** One relationship covers many rooftops (a venue operator, a district, a
   chain, an integrator). Single-site buyers are worth doing only as references.
4. **No declared incumbent capability.** "Declared" is the honest word — absence of a public
   announcement is not proof of absence in the building. Qualification question, not assumption:
   *who currently watches your roofline, and what do they see at 11pm?*
5. **A procurement path that skips a full RFP.** Co-op contract vehicles, sole-source pilot
   justification, event budget, or retainer. If it needs a 9-month public RFP, it is not a Q1 sale.

Score every account against these five. Three or fewer = nurture list, not a call list.

## 2. Why each segment buys (the actual logic)

| Segment | Why they buy | Why they stall |
|---|---|---|
| Venue operators / promoters (multi-site) | One sale = dozens to hundreds of rooftops; they carry the liability for every event | Central security standards take time to amend |
| Pro & college stadium owner-operators | Rooflines are the classic blind spot; staff can't watch every surface | Already bought entry screening → think they're covered |
| Motorsport / fairgrounds / rodeo | Open perimeters, temporary structures, real trespass + theft history | Seasonal budget cycles |
| K-12 districts (large) | Rooftops on every campus; safety budget is political and funded | Slow procurement unless they use a co-op vehicle |
| Universities (own police + big stadium) | Bespoke systems, real budgets, own PD can pilot without a public RFP | Committee decision-making |
| Protective-services firms (executive protection) | They resell/deploy for clients → **channel**, not end-user | Will want margin and white-label |
| Megachurches | 5k+ weekly gatherings, volunteer teams, donation budgets, incident-driven | Small staff, no technical buyer |
| Convention centers / political events | Rooftops around every host venue; host committees have event money | Money appears only in an event cycle |
| Data centers | Huge rooflines, existing 24/7 SOC, capital budget, multi-site | Incumbents (fence/thermal) already sold; must differentiate on mask + speed |
| Airports / FBOs / ports / rail | Asset theft and perimeter intrusion are operational facts | Procurement is slow and regulated; use integrators |
| Utilities / refineries / LNG | Regulated perimeter requirements | Nuclear-tier sites already have declared systems — skip those |
| Hospitals | Violence-against-staff pressure, many rooftops | Clinical budgets compete |

## 3. The channel play (do not ignore this)

Selling one box at a time to venues is the slowest possible path. Three faster ones:

- **Security integrators** (they already hold the contracts and the trucks): Convergint, Johnson
  Controls, Allied Universal Technology Services, Securitas Technology, ADT Commercial. One
  integrator partnership = a national account channel with install capability.
- **Protective-services firms**: they deploy to temporary venues for protectees. A portable,
  on-prem box fits their logistics model — and nothing leaves the site, which is the objection they
  always face.
- **Venue operators**: the multi-site leverage in §1.

## 5. Honest limits

- "Not already declared" = no *public* declaration found. It cannot mean certainty about what is
  installed inside a private venue. Every A-tier call opens with the qualification question.
- Latency and mask numbers are from our own hardware (15 Sept 2026) — real, but they describe a
  single-frame pipeline on one machine. Do not quote them as an SLA for a multi-camera site.
- This is a customer list and buying rationale. Nothing here is about monitoring people — the
  product watches roofs, and the sale is to organizations that own the roofs.
